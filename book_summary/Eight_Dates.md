## Eight Dates — by John Gottman, Julie Schwartz Gottman, Doug Abrams & Rachel Carlton Abrams
### Lasting love isn't found once; it's an ongoing conversation you have to keep scheduling.

**ELEVATOR SUMMARY**
A structured program of eight intentional dates, each built around one make-or-break topic that couples routinely avoid. The premise: most relationships fail not from lack of love but from never having certain essential conversations. The book gives you the questions and the how-to.

**CORE ARGUMENT**
The Gottmans argue that open-ended, curious conversation about core life domains is the engine of intimacy — and that couples systematically dodge exactly the topics that matter most (money, sex, family, mortality). By turning these into structured "dates" with prepared questions and conversational ground rules (be curious, ask open-ended questions, express your own needs without blame), couples can surface hidden expectations before they become silent landmines. The book leans on the idea that you never finish getting to know your partner; the "love maps" you build must keep updating.

**KEY IDEAS (the eight dates)**
1. **Trust & Commitment**: Trust is knowing your partner has your back; commitment is cherishing what you have rather than comparing it to alternatives.
2. **Conflict**: Conflict is an opportunity to understand, not to win — a preview of *Fight Right*'s thesis.
3. **Sex & Intimacy**: Couples who talk explicitly about sex have better sex; silence breeds mismatch.
4. **Work & Money**: Money conversations are really about values, security, and childhood scripts.
5. **Family**: Deciding whether/how to have children and negotiating in-laws and roles.
6. **Fun & Adventure**: Play is not frivolous — shared novelty and fun sustain long-term bonds.
7. **Growth & Spirituality**: How you'll keep growing as individuals and as a couple; shared meaning.
8. **Dreams**: Honoring each other's life dreams — the deepest layer of the Gottman method.

**NOTABLE EXAMPLES / CASE STUDIES**
- Each date includes real couples' dialogues and the specific open-ended questions to ask (e.g., "What does trust mean to you?").
- Practical staging advice: where to go, how to prepare, ground rules for listening.

**CRITICAL ANALYSIS**
*Strengths*: Exceptionally actionable — it's practically a workbook. The question sets alone are worth the price.
*Limitations*: Light on new theory; it repackages Gottman research into an activity format. Highly motivated couples benefit most; a reluctant partner can make the structure feel forced.
*Best for*: Couples at any stage wanting a low-friction way to deepen connection; pre-engagement couples especially.
*Skip if*: You're in acute crisis or dealing with contempt/abuse — you likely need a therapist first.
*Connects to*: *The Seven Principles*; *The Relationship Cure* (love maps).

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- Schedule the conversations; don't wait for them to "come up."
- Ask open-ended questions and follow with curiosity, not rebuttal.
- Revisit these topics periodically — answers change over a lifetime.

**FURTHER READING**: *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*; *Fight Right*.
