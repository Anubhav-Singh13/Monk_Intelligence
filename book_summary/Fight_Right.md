## Fight Right — by Julie Schwartz Gottman & John Gottman
### You can't stop fighting with the person you love — but you can learn to fight in a way that pulls you closer.

**ELEVATOR SUMMARY**
The Gottmans' 2024 book reframes conflict as not merely survivable but potentially generative. Since roughly 69% of relationship problems are *perpetual* (rooted in personality and values, never fully "solved"), the goal isn't to win or eliminate fights but to fight in ways that build understanding instead of damage.

**CORE ARGUMENT**
Drawing on decades of lab data, the Gottmans contend that *how* couples argue is one of the strongest predictors of whether they stay together and stay happy. Most conflict advice fails because it treats fights as problems to be solved; the Gottmans argue that beneath most recurring fights lie **enduring vulnerabilities** (old wounds, formative experiences) and **hidden dreams**. The path forward is to slow conflict down, avoid the "Four Horsemen" (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling), take physiological breaks when "flooded," and use conflict as a doorway into each other's deeper needs.

**KEY IDEAS**
1. **Perpetual vs. solvable problems**: Most fights are perpetual. Trying to permanently solve them is the mistake; managing them with humor and acceptance is the skill.
2. **Conflict cultures**: Couples differ in how much conflict feels normal (conflict-avoidant, validating, volatile). Trouble comes from *mismatch*, not from any one style.
3. **Enduring vulnerabilities**: Disproportionate reactions usually trace to old wounds. Recognizing them turns "why are you overreacting?" into "what got triggered?"
4. **Dreams within conflict**: Behind gridlocked positions are unspoken dreams and values. Uncovering them dissolves gridlock into dialogue.
5. **Flooding and repair**: When heart rate spikes, rational conversation is impossible — take a real break (20+ minutes) and return. **Repair attempts** (humor, apology, softening) are the lifeline of any fight.
6. **The soft start-up**: How you open a hard conversation (in the first three minutes) largely determines how it ends.

**NOTABLE EXAMPLES / CASE STUDIES**
- Transcripts of couples' fights annotated to show where the Four Horsemen appear and where repair could have happened.
- The "dreams within conflict" exercise applied to a gridlocked disagreement.

**CRITICAL ANALYSIS**
*Strengths*: Physiologically grounded (the flooding/heart-rate research is distinctive), and normalizes conflict in a way that reduces shame.
*Limitations*: Much overlaps with *The Seven Principles*; longtime Gottman readers will find it more consolidation than revelation. The "69%" statistic gets repeated as a near-mantra.
*Best for*: Couples who fight the same fight over and over and feel hopeless about it.
*Skip if*: Contempt has become pervasive or there's abuse — the book itself points you toward professional help.
*Connects to*: *The Seven Principles*; *Eight Dates* (the Conflict date).

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- Accept that some problems will never be solved — aim to dialogue about them without gridlock.
- Learn your flooding signs and call a timeout before you say something corrosive.
- Open hard talks softly; make and accept repair attempts.

**FURTHER READING**: *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*; *Why Marriages Succeed or Fail*.
