# Learning Path — Approved

## Design decisions

- **Scope:** the first four of the seven *bhūmikās* (stages of knowledge) from the Yoga Vasiṣṭha. Stages 5–7 (*asaṃsakti*, *padārthābhāvanā*, *turyagā*) describe a ripening, not a trainable skill; Day 38 tells you where to read about them.
- **Depth:** L2 — Builder. You reconstruct and defend the arguments, and apply them to live experience.
- **Rate-limiting sub-skill:** *dṛg-dṛśya-viveka* — discriminating the seer from the seen *as a live act*, not as an agreed idea; and, as its second face, a correct grasp of *mithyā*. Three drill days (9, 19, 32) target it directly; most pages embed one exercise on it.
- **Disagreements between sources:** the course foregrounds **convergence**. Every modern-teacher page maps its method back onto the classical seer/seen discrimination; differences appear as differences of *entry point*, and Day 30 states the shared invariants.
- **Unifying story:** the Tenth Man (Day 4) — the seeker is the sought.

## Arc narrative

On Day 1 you only need to notice something you already know: that your wanting never quite comes to rest. Nothing is asked on faith. By Day 13 you can take any experience apart into *what is seen* and *what sees*, explain why confusing the two is bondage, and say precisely why the world is *mithyā* — neither illusion nor ultimately real. By Day 20 you can run *tat tvam asi* and *neti neti* yourself. By Day 35 you will have practised four living doors — Ramana, Nisargadatta, Krishnamurti, the direct path — and seen that they converge on the discrimination the Upaniṣads taught.

The Tenth Man ties it together. *Shubheccha* is sensing someone is missing. *Vicāraṇā* is learning to count. *Tanumānasā* is quietening the panic so the counting can be done calmly. *Sattvāpatti* is the moment "you are the tenth" can no longer be unheard. The course ends there — at stable knowledge — with a map back into the texts.
