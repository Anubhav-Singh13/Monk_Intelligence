## 1. Foundational books — the spine

### Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Eight Upaniṣads, with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*, 2 vols. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1957–1958 (many reprints).
Covers Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Taittirīya (vol. 1) and Aitareya, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya with Gauḍapāda's Kārikā, Praśna (vol. 2), each with Shankara's full commentary. It favours a strictly Shankaran reading and translates the commentary literally, which makes it dense: the Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (especially chapters 3–4) is the hardest stretch. **Used most on:** Days 2 (Kaṭha 1.1–1.2), 3, 5 (Kena 1), 7 and 11 (Taittirīya 2), 8, 10 and 18 (Māṇḍūkya + Kārikā), 17 (Kena 2).

### Swami Swahananda (trans.), *Pañcadaśī of Śrī Vidyāraṇya Swāmī*. Chennai: Sri Ramakrishna Math, 1967.
Vidyāraṇya's fourteenth-century manual in fifteen chapters: five *vivekas* (discriminations), five *dīpas* (lamps), five *ānandas* (blisses). It is the most practical classical text — every doctrine is pressed into application — and it freely uses analogies (the tenth man, the painted canvas, the lamp on the stage). Hardest part: chapter 6 (*Citra-dīpa*) on Īśvara and the reflected consciousness. **Used most on:** Days 1 (ch. 15), 4 and 37 (ch. 7), 7 (ch. 3), 8 (ch. 1), 16 (ch. 5), 31, 33.

### Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Bhagavad Gītā with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1984.
The Gītā as Shankara reads it: knowledge alone liberates; action and devotion prepare the mind. Shankara's introduction and his commentary on 2.10–11 and 18.66 are the densest passages. **Used most on:** Days 2 (2.42–44), 21 (chs. 2–3), 22 (ch. 7, 12), 23 (ch. 6), 36 (2.55–72).

### Sengaku Mayeda (trans.), *A Thousand Teachings: The Upadeśasāhasrī of Śaṅkara*. Tokyo: University of Tokyo Press, 1979; reprinted Albany: SUNY Press, 1992.
Shankara's one undisputed independent work, in a prose part (how to teach a student, as a dialogue) and a verse part. Mayeda's long introduction is the best short account of Shankara's thought and of how scholars decide which works he actually wrote. Its intuition favours the teacher–student method; hardest part is the verse section's compressed arguments. **Used most on:** Days 3 (authorship question), 4, 12, 16, 17.

### Michael Comans, *The Method of Early Advaita Vedānta: A Study of Gauḍapāda, Śaṅkara, Sureśvara and Padmapāda*. Delhi: Motilal Banarsidass, 2000.
A scholar-practitioner's account of how the early teachers actually *taught*: the role of scripture, the three states, *neti neti*, and whether yoga-style meditation is required. It is the clearest bridge between the traditional and the "experiential" camps. Academic in style; the Gauḍapāda chapters are hardest. **Used most on:** Days 4, 20, 23, 30.

## 2. Primary-text translations — per-day sources

### Swami Madhavananda (trans.), *The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1934.
The largest Upaniṣad and Shankara's longest commentary. Yājñavalkya's dialogues (books 2–4) are the core. **Used on:** Day 5 (4.3.1–7, Janaka's question about light), 17 (2.3.6 *neti neti*; 3.4.2 the knower of knowing), 20 (2.4.5 *śrotavya, mantavya, nididhyāsitavya*).

### Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Chāndogya Upaniṣad with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1983.
**Used on:** Day 1 (7.1–7.2, 7.23–24, Nārada and Sanatkumāra: "the infinite is happiness"), 8 (ch. 8, Indra and Prajāpati), 13 (6.1.4, clay), 16 (6.8.7, *tat tvam asi*).

### Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Brahma-Sūtra-Bhāṣya of Śrī Śaṅkarācārya*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1965.
**Used on:** Day 3 (1.1.1, the qualifications implied by "atha"), 12 (the preamble, *Adhyāsa Bhāṣya*), 15 (1.1.2), 23 (1.1.4, knowledge as *vastu-tantra*).

### Swami Nikhilananda (trans.), *Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka: An Inquiry into the Nature of the 'Seer' and the 'Seen'*. Mysore: Sri Ramakrishna Ashrama, 1931.
A 46-verse primer; verse 1 is the course's rate-limiting skill in one line. **Used on:** Days 6, 9.

### Swami Madhavananda (trans.), *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi of Śrī Śaṅkarācārya*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1921.
Traditionally attributed to Shankara; most modern scholars (following Hacker and Mayeda's criteria) doubt the attribution. Still the most widely used introductory text. **Used on:** Days 3, 7.

### Swami Venkatesananda (trans.), *Vasiṣṭha's Yoga*. Albany: SUNY Press, 1993.
An abridged English rendering of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha. Source of the seven *bhūmikās* that frame this course — the stage list appears in the Utpatti Prakaraṇa **[VERIFY exact sarga in your edition]**. **Used on:** README framing, Day 38.

### Swami Nityaswarupananda (trans.), *Aṣṭāvakra Saṃhitā*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1940.
**Used on:** Day 34.

### Swami Moksadananda (trans.), *Jīvanmukti-viveka of Swami Vidyāraṇya*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama **[VERIFY year, c. 1996]**.
Vidyāraṇya's practical treatise on living liberation; its doctrine of the three practices (knowledge, dissolution of mind, wearing-away of *vāsanās*) is the core of Day 24. **Used on:** Days 24, 37.

### Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Bhagavad Gītā with the Annotation Gūḍhārtha-Dīpikā of Madhusūdana Sarasvatī*. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama, 1998.
Madhusūdana's synthesis of strict Advaita with devotion. **Used on:** Day 22.

### A. J. Alston (trans.), *The Realization of the Absolute: The "Naiṣkarmya Siddhi" of Śrī Sureśvara*. London: Shanti Sadan, 1959.
Sureśvara's argument that knowledge, not action, liberates. **Used on:** Days 16, 31.

### D. Venkataramiah (trans.), *The Pañcapādikā of Padmapāda*. Baroda: Gaekwad's Oriental Series, 1948.
Optional depth on *adhyāsa*. **Used on:** Day 12.

## 3. Modern teachers

### Munagala Venkataramiah (recorder), *Talks with Sri Ramana Maharshi*. Tiruvannamalai: Sri Ramanasramam, 1955. — and Ramana Maharshi, *Who Am I? (Nān Yār?)*, Sri Ramanasramam (many editions).
*Nān Yār?* is a few pages long and should be read in full on Day 26. **Used on:** Day 26.

### Nisargadatta Maharaj, *I Am That*, trans. Maurice Frydman, ed. Sudhakar S. Dikshit. Bombay: Chetana, 1973.
Dialogues; no systematic order. **Used on:** Day 27.

### Nitya Tripta (recorder), *Notes on Spiritual Discourses of Shri Atmananda*. 1963; reprinted Salisbury: Non-Duality Press, 2009.
The fullest record of Atmananda Krishna Menon's "direct path." **Used on:** Day 29.

### Swami Satchidanandendra Saraswati, *The Method of the Vedanta: A Critical Account of the Advaita Tradition*, trans. A. J. Alston. London: Kegan Paul International, 1989.
A strict return to Shankara's own words; argues that later Advaita misread *avidyā*. **Used on:** Days 12, 30.

### Swami Dayananda Saraswati, *Introduction to Vedanta: Understanding the Fundamental Problem*. New Delhi: Vision Books, 1989.
The clearest modern statement of the "human problem" as a sense of limitation and of Vedānta as a means of knowledge. **Used on:** Days 1, 4, 20, 21.

### James Swartz, *How to Attain Enlightenment: The Vision of Non-Duality*. Boulder: Sentient Publications, 2009.
Traditional Vedānta in contemporary Western language; strong on the knowledge-vs-experience distinction. **Used on:** Days 30, 33.

### Rupert Spira, *The Nature of Consciousness: Essays on the Unity of Mind and Matter*. Oxford: Sahaja Publications / Oakland: New Harbinger, 2017.
The direct path in contemporary philosophical prose. **Used on:** Days 29, 32.

### J. Krishnamurti, *The First and Last Freedom*. New York: Harper & Row, 1954. — and J. Krishnamurti & David Bohm, *The Ending of Time*. San Francisco: Harper & Row, 1985.
**Used on:** Day 28.

### Swami Vivekananda, *Jnana Yoga*, in *The Complete Works of Swami Vivekananda*, Vol. 2. Kolkata: Advaita Ashrama.
Lectures delivered in London and New York (1896); Advaita for a modern Western audience. **Used on:** Days 1, 15.

### Francis Lucille, *Eternity Now* **[VERIFY publisher/year]**.
**Used on:** Day 29.

### Swami Chinmayananda, *Vivekachoodamani* (commentary). Mumbai: Central Chinmaya Mission Trust **[VERIFY edition]**.
**Used on:** Day 3.

## 4. Scholarly papers and monographs

### Daniel H. H. Ingalls, "Śaṃkara on the Question: Whose is Avidyā?", *Philosophy East and West* 3(1), 1953, pp. 69–72.
Short and sharp: Shankara refuses to locate ignorance in Brahman or in a real individual. **Used on:** Days 12, 30.

### Sengaku Mayeda, "The Authenticity of the Upadeśasāhasrī Ascribed to Śaṅkara", *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 85(2), 1965 **[VERIFY pages]**.
The method scholars use to decide what Shankara wrote — context for the Vivekacūḍāmaṇi debate. **Used on:** Day 3.

### Paul Hacker, "Eigentümlichkeiten der Lehre und Terminologie Śaṅkaras: Avidyā, Nāmarūpa, Māyā, Īśvara", *Zeitschrift der Deutschen Morgenländischen Gesellschaft* 100, 1950 **[VERIFY pages]**. (German.)
Shankara's distinctive use of *avidyā*, *māyā* and *Īśvara*. **Used on:** Day 15.

### Anantanand Rambachan, *Accomplishing the Accomplished: The Vedas as a Source of Valid Knowledge in Śaṅkara*. Honolulu: University of Hawaii Press, 1991.
Argues that for Shankara the Upaniṣad is the *pramāṇa* (means of knowledge) for Brahman — not a record of mystical experience. **Used on:** Days 4, 30.

### Wilhelm Halbfass, "The Concept of Experience in the Encounter between India and the West", in *India and Europe: An Essay in Understanding*. Albany: SUNY Press, 1988.
How "experience" entered modern Hindu self-description (Vivekananda onward). Explains why teachers *sound* more opposed than they are. **Used on:** Day 30.

### Robert H. Sharf, "Buddhist Modernism and the Rhetoric of Meditative Experience", *Numen* 42(3), 1995, pp. 228–283.
The Buddhist parallel to Halbfass — relevant to your Buddhist background. **Used on:** Day 30.

### Miri Albahari, "Perennial Idealism: A Mystical Solution to the Mind-Body Problem", *Philosophers' Imprint* 19(44), 2019.
A contemporary analytic philosopher defending an Advaita-like view against physicalism. **Used on:** Day 32, capstone.

### Evan Thompson, *Waking, Dreaming, Being: Self and Consciousness in Neuroscience, Meditation, and Philosophy*. New York: Columbia University Press, 2015.
Engages the Māṇḍūkya's four states with sleep science; sympathetic but critical. **Used on:** Days 8, 10.
