# Aham Brahmāsmi — A First-Principles Course in Advaita Vedānta

> **The promise:** In 39 days you will be able to rebuild, from first principles and without notes, the argument that "I am Brahman" — and you will have practised the core discrimination (seer vs. seen) often enough that it begins to operate in live experience, not just on paper.

## Who this course is for

You are an intelligent adult who has met Advaita and Buddhist thought, meditates, and has read many of the scriptures in English with commentary. What you lack is not exposure but *structure*: a single first-principles spine that shows how the pieces (the three states, the five sheaths, adhyāsa, mithyā, the mahāvākyas, the modern methods) lock together into one argument, and a training regime that turns that argument into a working understanding. The course targets **L2 — Builder**: you will reconstruct the arguments yourself, defend them against objections, and apply them to your own experience.

It optimises for one outcome: **a working understanding of *aham brahmāsmi* and the first steps of seeing it for yourself.** It covers the first four of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's seven bhūmikās (stages) — the ones that can be trained. It is *not* for someone wanting a history of Indian philosophy, a Sanskrit course, or a guarantee of enlightenment. It is also not a replacement for the scriptures: it gives you the concepts so that you can read the texts yourself afterwards (Day 38 gives you a 12-month reading plan).

## The arc at a glance

On Day 1 you only need to notice something you already know: that your wanting never quite comes to rest. Nothing is asked on faith. By Day 13 you can take any experience apart into *what is seen* and *what sees*, explain why confusing the two is bondage, and say precisely why the world is *mithyā* — neither illusion nor ultimately real. By Day 20 you can run the great equation *tat tvam asi* and the method of *neti neti* yourself. By Day 35 you will have practised four living doors — Ramana, Nisargadatta, Krishnamurti, and the direct path — and seen that they converge on the same discrimination the Upaniṣads taught.

The thread is the **Tenth Man**. Ten men cross a river, each counts the others and forgets himself, and they grieve for a man who was never lost. *Shubheccha* (stage 1) is sensing that someone is missing. *Vicāraṇā* (stage 2) is learning to count. *Tanumānasā* (stage 3) is quietening the panic so the counting can be done calmly. *Sattvāpatti* (stage 4) is the moment "you are the tenth" can no longer be unheard. Shankara, Ramana, Nisargadatta, Atmananda and even Krishnamurti are all pointing at the one person who forgot to count himself.

## How to use this course

- **Rhythm:** one page per day, 35–45 minutes of focused reading and exercises (Module 1 is lighter; Modules 2–4 sit near 45). Same time each day if possible.
- **Exercises are non-optional.** This is an L2 course. The first exercise on every page is a closed-book retrieval — do it *before* scrolling back. The "direct application" exercise is the one that makes the teaching yours.
- **Bibliography:** consult [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md) only when a page's "Suggested readings" points you there, or when you have spare time. Do not let the reading list slow your daily rhythm — the scriptures come properly after Day 38.
- **Rest & Synthesize days (14, 25, 35):** no new material. You retrieve from memory first, then re-study only what retrieval showed was shaky.
- **Drill days (9, 19, 32):** a gym, not a library. Isolated, progressively harder exercises on the bottleneck skill — seer/seen discrimination and its applications. Discomfort is the signal it is working.
- **Sufi & Bhakti convergence boxes:** on 13 days (1, 4, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22, 23, 26, 36) a short box shows the same insight reached through love rather than enquiry — Rūmī, Ibn ʿArabī, al-Ḥallāj, Kabīr, Jñāneśvar, Tukārām, Lal Ded, Bulleh Shah, Dārā Shikōh — with one honest line on where they differ. Day 30's matrix adds Sufi and nirguṇa-bhakti rows; Day 32 adds a Sufi objector (Sirhindī). Quotations are in English; sources are in [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md) §7.
- **If life gets in the way:** never skip a day's retrieval exercise; if you miss days, restart with the "Before you start" prompt of the next page and do the missed page's exercise 1 only. Don't binge two pages in one sitting.

## The Course Shelf

The five sources that carry most of the weight (full annotated list in [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md)):

1. **Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Eight Upaniṣads with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*** — the primary texts and Shankara's reading of them, in one place.
2. **Swami Swahananda (trans.), *Pañcadaśī of Śrī Vidyāraṇya*** — the most practical classical manual; spine for the sheaths, states, tenth man and obstacles.
3. **Swami Gambhirananda (trans.), *Bhagavad Gītā with the Commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*** — the preparation (karma yoga, bhakti, meditation) and the portrait of the steady knower.
4. **Sengaku Mayeda (trans.), *A Thousand Teachings: The Upadeśasāhasrī of Śaṅkara*** — Shankara's own teaching method, in his undisputed independent work.
5. **Michael Comans, *The Method of Early Advaita Vedānta*** — the scholarly map of how Gauḍapāda, Shankara, Sureśvara and Padmapāda actually taught, and of the knowledge-vs-experience question.

## The capstone

Explain *aham brahmāsmi* from first principles to a real, sceptical listener — a friend or your meditation group — in 20 minutes, without notes: seer/seen → adhyāsa → mithyā → mahāvākya. Answer three objections live: a Buddhist's *anātman*, a Dvaitin's "the soul and God are eternally distinct", and a physicalist's "consciousness is what the brain does". Show how two different doors you have practised (choose from Shankara's equation, Ramana's enquiry, the direct path) arrive at the same recognition. Keep a 7-day *nididhyāsana* log alongside. **Done** = your listener can restate the argument back to you, and you never had to fall back on "you just have to experience it."
