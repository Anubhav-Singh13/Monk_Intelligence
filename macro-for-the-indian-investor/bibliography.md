# Bibliography — The Course Shelf

The course is not freestyle. Each page is sequenced and distilled from the works below. The **global spine** supplies the universal mechanism (how any economy and market work); the **India layer** supplies the instruments, data, and case studies that make it real for a rupee investor.

Citation discipline: every entry is a work I am confident exists. Anything I could not verify with confidence is marked `[VERIFY]` for you to check before relying on it. Where a source is a living document (RBI, MOSPI), the URL is the stable landing page, not a dated PDF.

## Foundational books

### Global spine

**Ray Dalio — *Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises*. Bridgewater / self-published, 2018.**
Dalio's "economic machine" template: credit cycles, deleveragings, and the four levers policymakers pull. Favours a mechanical, cash-flow intuition over equations. Hardest in the detailed case studies (Part 3) — skim those. *Relied on most: Days 14 (transmission), 21 (regimes), 25 (breakage).*

**N. Gregory Mankiw — *Macroeconomics*. 10th ed., Worth Publishers, 2019.**
The intuition-forward standard undergraduate text. Best-in-class at building a concept from a picture before the algebra. Favours the mainstream (New Keynesian) consensus. Hardest chapters are the open-economy and IS-LM ones — we take only the intuition. *Spine for Days 4, 6, 9, 10, 13.*

**Olivier Blanchard — *Macroeconomics*. 8th ed., Pearson, 2021.**
A more rigorous companion to Mankiw with an unusually clear treatment of expectations and the yield curve. Favours the "medium-run vs. short-run" framing this course leans on. *Alternative intuition for Days 6, 11, 17.*

**Antti Ilmanen — *Expected Returns: An Investor's Guide to Harvesting Market Rewards*. Wiley, 2011.**
The bridge from macro to what assets actually *pay*. Treats returns as compensation for bearing macro risks (growth, inflation, illiquidity). Dense — read the chapter intros and the summary tables. *Spine for the assets arc, Days 17, 20, 21.*

**George Cooper — *The Origin of Financial Crises*. Harriman House, 2008.**
A short, sharp argument that markets are inherently unstable (Minsky + reflexivity), against the efficient-markets default. *Primary source for Day 25.*

**Daniel Kahneman — *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011.**
System 1/System 2, anchoring, availability, and prospect theory — the machinery of how humans misjudge probability and risk. *Spine for Day 18; background for Days 20, 22.*

**Robert J. Shiller — *Irrational Exuberance*. 3rd ed., Princeton University Press, 2015.**
The definitive treatment of feedback loops and bubbles, with the CAPE valuation lens. *Core of Day 25; supports Day 18.*

### India layer

**D. Subbarao — *Who Moved My Interest Rate? Leading the Reserve Bank of India Through Five Turbulent Years*. Penguin Random House India, 2016.**
An RBI Governor's inside account (2008–2013) of how Indian monetary policy is actually made under pressure — the GFC, the 2013 taper tantrum, the growth-vs-inflation fights with North Block. *Spine for Days 5, 13, 14, 25.*

**Raghuram G. Rajan — *I Do What I Do*. HarperCollins India, 2017.**
Speeches and essays from Rajan's RBI governorship, including the rationale for flexible inflation targeting and the defence of the rupee in 2013. *Relied on for Days 19, 23, 25.*

**Urjit Patel — *Overdraft: Saving the Indian Saver*. HarperCollins India, 2020.**
The banking- and NBFC-fragility story behind India's credit system, from the architect of inflation targeting. *Supports Days 14, 20.*

**Vijay Joshi — *India's Long Road: The Search for Prosperity*. Oxford University Press, 2017.**
The clearest single account of India's structural growth story and its constraints. *Supports Days 2, 7, 16.*

**Santosh Nair — *Bulls, Bears and Other Beasts: A Story of the Indian Stock Market*. Pan Macmillan India, 2018.**
A narrative history of Indian markets from Harshad Mehta to the present — crowd behaviour made concrete. *Supports Days 18, 25.*

## Landmark papers & primary documents

### Global spine

**Milton Friedman — "The Role of Monetary Policy." *American Economic Review* 58(1), 1968, pp. 1–17.**
Argues monetary policy can't hold unemployment below its natural rate for long; introduces expectations into the Phillips curve. *Days 13, 25.*

**John B. Taylor — "Discretion versus Policy Rules in Practice." *Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy* 39, 1993, pp. 195–214.**
The Taylor rule: set the policy rate as a function of inflation and the output gap. *Core of Day 13.*

**Ben S. Bernanke & Mark Gertler — "Inside the Black Box: The Credit Channel of Monetary Policy Transmission." *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 9(4), 1995, pp. 27–48.**
How rate changes reach the real economy through bank lending and balance sheets — not just the textbook interest-rate channel. *Core of Day 14.*

**Ben S. Bernanke & Kenneth N. Kuttner — "What Explains the Stock Market's Reaction to Federal Reserve Policy?" *Journal of Finance* 60(3), 2005, pp. 1221–1257.**
Decomposes equity reactions to Fed moves and shows the market responds to the *surprise* component, mostly via the equity risk premium. *Anchor for Days 3, 22, 23.*

**Arturo Estrella & Frederic S. Mishkin — "The Yield Curve as a Predictor of U.S. Recessions." Federal Reserve Bank of New York, *Current Issues in Economics and Finance* 2(7), 1996.**
The evidence that curve inversion precedes recessions. *Core of Day 11.*

**Eugene F. Fama — "Stock Returns, Real Activity, Inflation, and Money." *American Economic Review* 71(4), 1981, pp. 545–565.**
The messy, often negative real-world link between inflation and stock returns. *Days 9, 21.*

**Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman — "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases." *Science* 185(4157), 1974, pp. 1124–1131.**
The founding catalogue of anchoring, availability, and representativeness. *Day 18.*

**Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky — "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk." *Econometrica* 47(2), 1979, pp. 263–291.**
Loss aversion and the value function — why fear and greed are asymmetric. *Days 18, 20.*

**Werner F. M. De Bondt & Richard Thaler — "Does the Stock Market Overreact?" *Journal of Finance* 40(3), 1985, pp. 793–805.**
Direct evidence that prices over- and undershoot. *Days 18, 24.*

### India layer (primary documents)

**Reserve Bank of India — "Report of the Expert Committee to Revise and Strengthen the Monetary Policy Framework" (Chair: Urjit R. Patel). RBI, January 2014.**
The blueprint for India's shift to flexible inflation targeting and the CPI 4% (±2%) target. *Core of Day 13.* rbi.org.in.

**Reserve Bank of India — *Monetary Policy Report* (biannual, April & October). rbi.org.in.**
The RBI's own published reasoning: its inflation and growth projections and their drivers. *Days 10, 13, 23.*

**Government of India, Ministry of Finance — *Economic Survey* (annual). indiabudget.gov.in.**
The Chief Economic Adviser's official read on growth, fiscal position, and the external sector, published before the Union Budget. *Days 7, 16, 19.*

**Reserve Bank of India — MPC Resolutions, Minutes & Governor's Statements (bi-monthly). rbi.org.in.**
The primary market-moving documents parsed on Day 23 and used in the capstone.

## Video lectures / courses

**Ray Dalio — "How the Economic Machine Works." YouTube, 2013 (~31 min).**
The best single intuition primer in existence for this course. *Watch alongside Days 1–5.* Search title on YouTube (official Principles channel).

**Ben S. Bernanke — "The Federal Reserve and the Financial Crisis" (4 lectures at George Washington University). Federal Reserve, 2012.**
The central bank explained by the man who ran it through a crisis. *Days 13, 14, 25.* Published on federalreserve.gov.

**MIT OpenCourseWare — 14.02 *Principles of Macroeconomics*.**
Full undergraduate lecture set; the fallback when any formalism day doesn't click. ocw.mit.edu.

**FOMC & RBI MPC press conferences (ongoing).** Primary-source practice for Day 23 — watch one of each and compare how each central bank signals.

## Optional deeper dives

- **Robert J. Shiller — *Narrative Economics*. Princeton University Press, 2019.** How stories go viral and move the economy. *Day 25.*
- **George Soros — *The Alchemy of Finance*. Wiley, 1987 (rev. 2003).** Reflexivity from the practitioner who traded it. *Day 25.*
- **Richard H. Thaler — *Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics*. W. W. Norton, 2015.** The most readable overview of the field. *Day 18.*
- **Richard C. Koo — *The Escape from Balance Sheet Recession and the QE Trap*. Wiley, 2014.** Why economies get stuck after credit busts. *Day 25.*
- **Perry Mehrling — "Economics of Money and Banking." Coursera (Barnard/Columbia).** The plumbing behind Days 10 and 14.
