# Macro for the Indian Investor

> **The promise:** After 27 days you will be able to pick up a fresh Indian data release and RBI policy statement, separate the *surprise* from the *expected*, trace it through the transmission mechanism, and say a defensible sentence about what it means for the Nifty, G-secs, and the rupee — and spot when the tidy narrative is about to blow up.

## Who this course is for

You invest, you follow equities, and you are tired of macro news feeling like weather that happens *to* your portfolio — a CPI print, an RBI decision, a Fed meeting, a move in the rupee — where you can see the market react but not *why*. This course rebuilds macroeconomics as a **machine you can reason about**: a loop of spending, steered by two levers (the RBI and the Government), whose every move transmits through a knowable chain to asset prices.

It targets **depth level L1 — Practitioner**: you will learn to *use* macro to reason about markets, not to build econometric models or read the academic frontier. It assumes you are comfortable with equities but treats **interest rates, bonds, FX, and formal economics as fresh**. It optimises for a single outcome: **understanding why markets move on macro news so you stop being surprised.** It is *not* for someone who wants to forecast GDP to two decimal places or trade intraday — the payoff here is judgement, not point forecasts.

## The arc at a glance

On Day 1 you can read "GDP rose 6%" or "the RBI held the repo rate" and feel the market's reaction is essentially random. The course rebuilds macro as a *system*: a loop of transactions (Days 1–8), measured honestly (Days 6–9), steered by two levers (Days 10–16), whose every move transmits through a knowable chain to bond yields, equity multiples, the rupee, and credit spreads (Days 14–21). The connective tissue — introduced on Day 3 and hammered on every drill day — is that markets trade *expectations*, so what moves price is the **surprise**, and the surprise only bites through a **mechanism**. Behavioural economics (Day 18 onward) then explains the *gap*: real humans form expectations by anchoring and herding, so price systematically over- and undershoots what the mechanism alone would predict.

By Day 27 you can take a real, recent Indian data set and the latest MPC statement, identify what was surprising versus expected, trace the surprise through the chain, state a view on stocks, bonds, and the rupee — and, just as important, recognise when the regime has shifted and "rate cuts are bullish" is about to cost someone money. The single story tying it all together: **price moves on the gap between what happened and what was expected — and only in the direction the mechanism allows, distorted by the mood of the crowd.**

## How to use this course

- **Rhythm:** one page per day, 30–45 minutes of focused reading. Do not binge — the spacing is load-bearing.
- **Exercises are not optional.** Each page ends with a *retrieval-first* exercise: close the page and reconstruct the idea from memory before doing anything else. This single habit is worth more than re-reading the whole course.
- **The `Before you start` prompt** at the top of each page asks you to recall yesterday's idea cold. Do it. If you can't, that is your signal to revisit — not to plough on.
- **Rest & Synthesize days (12, 26)** add no new material. You reconstruct the arc from memory, *then* re-study only what your memory got wrong. Treat them as diagnostics, not days off.
- **Drill days (15, 24)** are the gym. Five-to-eight isolated reps on the one sub-skill that decides whether this course works for you: chaining a macro shock to an asset move. Expect discomfort.
- **`bibliography.md`** is for when a page genuinely doesn't click or you want the deep version — not something to consult mid-page. Keep moving; the "Suggested readings" section tells you exactly when a detour is worth it.
- **If life gets in the way:** skipping a day is fine; skipping the retrieval exercises is not. Better to do 4 pages a week properly than 7 passively.

## The Course Shelf

Five must-haves; the full annotated list is in [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md).

1. **Ray Dalio, "How the Economic Machine Works" (video, 2013)** — the single best 31-minute intuition primer; watch alongside Days 1–5.
2. **N. Gregory Mankiw, *Macroeconomics* (10th ed., 2019)** — the intuition-forward spine for the measurement arc.
3. **D. Subbarao, *Who Moved My Interest Rate?* (2016)** — an RBI Governor on how Indian monetary policy actually gets made.
4. **Antti Ilmanen, *Expected Returns* (2011)** — the bridge from macro to what assets actually pay.
5. **Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011)** — why real expectations, and therefore prices, misbehave.

## The capstone

On Day 27 you take a genuinely recent Indian macro moment — the latest CPI and GDP prints, the newest MPC resolution, the current 10-year G-sec yield and USD/INR — and write a one-page thesis: what surprised, why, how it transmits, what it means for equities/bonds/the rupee, and where your view could be wrong. "Done" means a colleague could read it and act on it without you in the room.
