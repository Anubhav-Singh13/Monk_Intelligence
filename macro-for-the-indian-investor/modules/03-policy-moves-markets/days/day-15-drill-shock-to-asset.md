# Day 15 — Drill I: Shock → Asset Move

> **Today: the gym, not the library.** No new concepts. Seven progressively harder reps of the one skill this whole course exists to build: taking a macro shock and chaining it — through the mechanism — to a specific move in four assets: **the Nifty (equities), the 10-year G-sec (bonds), USD/INR (the rupee), and credit spreads.**
> **Reading time:** ~45 min · **Prereqs:** [Days 6–14](../overview.md)
> **Before you start:** Recall Day 14 in one sentence, no looking — *name three transmission channels and which is fastest.*

**How to do this drill (read once, then do it honestly):**
For each shock, before opening the solution, write down — on paper — the **direction** (↑/↓) and a **one-line mechanism** for each of the four assets. Rules:
- You must state the *mechanism*, not just the direction. "Nifty down" scores zero; "Nifty down because higher discount rate lowers PV of future earnings" scores full.
- Do **not** peek at the solution until you've committed all four. The retrieval *is* the training.
- If your direction is right but your mechanism is wrong, mark it **wrong**. A right answer for the wrong reason will lose you money on the next, slightly different shock.

The four-asset scorecard to fill each time:

| Asset | Direction (↑/↓) | Your one-line mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Nifty (equities) | | |
| 10-year G-sec (bond price) | | |
| USD/INR (rupee) | | |
| Credit spreads | | |

*(Note on USD/INR: "↑" means the number goes up = **rupee weakens**. "Spreads ↑" = credit gets riskier/wider.)*

---

### Rep 1 — The clean one (warm-up)

**Shock:** CPI prints at 6.5%, well *above* both the 4% target and the market's expectation. Growth is fine.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
Higher-than-expected inflation → reaction function (Day 13) says the RBI hikes more than priced → work each asset off "expected rates just went up."
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Nifty | ↓ | Higher expected repo → higher discount rate → lower PV of future earnings; growth/rate-sensitives hit hardest. |
| G-sec price | ↓ | Higher expected rates → yields up → existing bond prices fall inversely (Day 10). |
| USD/INR | ↓ (rupee *stronger*, modestly) | Higher Indian rates make rupee assets more attractive to carry → some INR support — *but* see Rep 5 for when this flips. |
| Credit spreads | ↑ (slightly) | Tighter policy → tighter financial conditions → mild risk-off widens spreads. |

**The core chain:** inflation surprise → RBI hikes more than priced → the whole rate ladder up → the discount rate up → bonds down, equities down. Burn this in; it's the backbone of most reps.
</details>

---

### Rep 2 — The dovish surprise

**Shock:** The RBI holds the repo rate, but the Governor's statement turns unexpectedly *dovish* — signalling cuts are coming sooner than the market thought. The rate itself didn't change.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
Nothing changed in the *action* — so what moved? The *expectations* channel (Day 14, fastest) and the *surprise* (Day 3). Work off "expected future rates just fell."
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Nifty | ↑ | Lower expected future rates → lower discount rate → higher PV of earnings; rate-sensitives rally most. |
| G-sec price | ↑ | Expected future short rates fall → long yields fall → bond prices rise (Day 11's expectations logic). |
| USD/INR | ↑ (rupee weaker) | Lower expected Indian rates → less carry appeal → mild INR softening. |
| Credit spreads | ↓ (tighter) | Easier expected policy → risk-on → spreads compress. |

**The lesson of this rep:** the rate *didn't move* and yet everything did — because markets trade the **surprise** and the **expected path**, not the action (Day 3, Day 14). If you wrote "no change, nothing moves," re-read Day 3.
</details>

---

### Rep 3 — The oil shock (two forces collide)

**Shock:** Global crude oil jumps 25% on a geopolitical event. Nothing else changes yet.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
India imports ~85% of its oil. Trace *two* separate effects: the trade/rupee effect (Day 7 NX, Day 19) and the inflation effect (Day 4 cost-push → Day 13 RBI). They compound.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| USD/INR | ↓↓ (rupee weaker) | Higher oil bill → more dollars demanded to pay for imports → NX worsens (Day 7) → rupee falls. |
| G-sec price | ↓ | Higher imported inflation → RBI likely to stay tight/hike (Day 13) → yields up → prices down. |
| Nifty | ↓ (mixed) | Higher input costs squeeze margins broadly; higher rates lift discount rate. *But* oil producers (ONGC) and exporters may rise — index net down. |
| Credit spreads | ↑ | Growth worry + tighter conditions → risk-off widens spreads. |

**The lesson:** one shock, *multiple channels* firing at once (rupee + inflation + margins). The sophisticated read names all of them and the *net*. Note the within-equity divergence — the index falls but not every stock (upstream oil gains).
</details>

---

### Rep 4 — The fiscal surprise

**Shock:** The Union Budget announces a *much larger* fiscal deficit than expected — a big spending/borrowing programme.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
Two effects on bonds pull the same way: more bond *supply* (Day 10) and higher expected inflation/RBI response (Day 13). Think about who has to buy all those new bonds.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| G-sec price | ↓ | More borrowing = flood of new bond *supply* → prices down, yields up (Day 10); plus higher expected inflation and a hawkish RBI lean (Day 13). |
| Nifty | ↕ (mixed) | Near-term positive for spending-linked sectors (infra, capex beneficiaries of G); negative via higher rates/discount rate and crowding-out worry. Often initially up on the growth impulse, capped by the yield rise. |
| USD/INR | ↓ (rupee weaker) | Fiscal profligacy → inflation/credibility worry, higher CAD risk → rupee softens. |
| Credit spreads | ↑ | Higher G-sec yields raise the whole cost-of-capital base; crowding out can widen corporate spreads. |

**The lesson:** the two levers can *fight* (Day 5) — loose fiscal + the RBI's inflation mandate = bonds get hit from both sides. Equities are genuinely ambiguous here; naming the tension is the correct answer, not forcing a single direction.
</details>

---

### Rep 5 — The global shock (the rupee flips)

**Shock:** The US Fed unexpectedly signals aggressive hikes. US yields jump. The RBI has done nothing.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
This is external. Think about FII flows (Day 19 preview): where does global money go when US rates rise? Contrast with Rep 1, where *higher rates supported* the rupee — here higher *foreign* rates hurt it.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| USD/INR | ↓↓ (rupee weaker) | Higher US yields → global money flows *out* of EM assets toward the safer, now higher-yielding dollar → FII outflows → rupee falls. |
| Nifty | ↓ | FII selling of Indian equities (funding the rotation to the US) + a weaker rupee/higher imported inflation → down. |
| G-sec price | ↓ | FII outflows from bonds + pressure on the RBI to hike (defend the rupee) → yields up, prices down. |
| Credit spreads | ↑ | Global risk-off → EM spreads widen. |

**The lesson — the false friend:** in Rep 1, higher *Indian* rates *helped* the rupee (more carry). Here, higher *US* rates *hurt* the rupee (capital flight). "Higher rates → stronger currency" is only true for *domestic* rate rises, holding the world constant. *Whose* rates rose changes the sign. This is exactly the mechanism-vs-narrative trap Day 24 drills hardest.
</details>

---

### Rep 6 — The ambiguous growth-driven rate rise

**Shock:** G-sec yields rise 40 bp — but the cause is a string of *strong* growth data (hot GDP, booming PMI), not an inflation scare. The RBI hasn't moved.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
Rates up usually hurts equities — but *why* did they rise? "Good rates up" (growth) vs. "bad rates up" (inflation/fiscal) can push equities in *opposite* directions. Weigh the discount-rate drag against the earnings boost.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| G-sec price | ↓ | Yields rose (strong growth → market expects the RBI to tighten) → prices down. Unambiguous. |
| Nifty | ↑ (likely) | Here the *cause* matters: rates rose because *growth is strong*, so the earnings boost (higher E) can outweigh the higher discount rate. Cyclicals lead. |
| USD/INR | ↑ (rupee stronger) | Strong growth + higher rates → attracts inflows → rupee supported. |
| Credit spreads | ↓ (tighter) | Strong growth → lower default risk → spreads compress *despite* higher base yields. |

**The lesson:** "rates up → stocks down" is a *narrative*, not a law. When rates rise for *growth* reasons, equities and credit can rally even as bonds fall. Always ask **why** rates moved before applying the reflex. If you wrote "Nifty down" mechanically, you just fell for the exact trap Day 24 exists to kill.
</details>

---

### Rep 7 — The full scenario (put it all together)

**Shock:** A poor monsoon spikes food prices; CPI jumps to 6.8% (above the band). *Simultaneously*, global oil rises 15%, and growth data is already *soft*. The market had priced the RBI on hold.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>
Multiple forces, some conflicting. Separate: (a) does the RBI hike into weak growth, or look through a supply shock (Day 9, Day 13)? (b) the rupee faces oil pressure; (c) equities face margin + growth + rate pressures. State the *tension* and give a base case.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

**First, resolve the RBI dilemma:** inflation is supply-driven (food + oil, cost-push) *and* growth is soft — the reaction function is conflicted (Day 13). Base case: the RBI turns *hawkish in tone* (to anchor expectations) but may hold, watching whether it leaks into core. High uncertainty.

| Asset | Dir | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| G-sec price | ↓ | Inflation above band + oil → yields up on inflation/hawkish-tone risk, even if the RBI holds. |
| USD/INR | ↓↓ (rupee weaker) | Oil import bill + inflation + soft growth → rupee under clear pressure (the one high-conviction call). |
| Nifty | ↓ | Triple hit: margin squeeze (food/oil costs), soft growth (weak earnings), higher discount rate. Defensives outperform cyclicals. |
| Credit spreads | ↑ | Stagflation-ish mix (weak growth + high inflation) is the worst backdrop for credit → spreads widen. |

**The lesson:** real scenarios are *messy* — conflicting forces, an ambiguous RBI. The professional answer isn't false precision; it's (1) name every force, (2) identify the highest-conviction call (here, the rupee), (3) state the key uncertainty (does the food shock leak into core?), and (4) give a base case anyway. That structure *is* the skill.
</details>

## Debrief (2 min)

Score yourself: **7/7 mechanisms correct** = the core skill is landing. **4–6** = solid, revisit the reps you missed and re-read the linked day. **Below 4** = the mechanism isn't automatic yet; re-read Days 10, 13, 14 before Module 4, because everything ahead assumes this chain is reflexive.

The three traps this drill targeted, which return on Day 24:
1. **"Nothing changed" when the rate held** (Reps 2, 7) — you must trade the *surprise* and the *expected path*, not the action.
2. **The rupee sign flip** (Rep 1 vs. Rep 5) — *whose* rates moved decides the direction.
3. **"Rates up → stocks down" as a reflex** (Rep 6) — *why* rates moved decides whether equities rise or fall.

> **Transfer — apply it:** Take the single most recent macro headline you've seen (today's news), and run it through the four-asset scorecard from scratch — direction *and* mechanism for each. If you can do it cold, in under three minutes, you're carrying the core skill into the real world. That is exactly what Day 27's capstone will ask, on live data.
