# Day 27 — Capstone: Your Macro Thesis

> **Today's one task:** Take *real, current* Indian macro data and the latest RBI statement, and produce a one-page, mechanism-based market thesis that a colleague could read and act on — proving you can operate the whole machine, not just describe it.
> **Reading time:** ~45–60 min (this is a *doing* day) · **Prereqs:** the entire course
> **Primary source for today:** live RBI (rbi.org.in), MOSPI (mospi.gov.in), and market data.
> **Before you start:** Recall the seven-part thesis structure from [Day 26](day-26-rest-and-synthesize-2.md), Step 3 — no looking.

## Why this is the whole point

Everything so far has been rehearsal. A macro understanding you can't *apply to today's data* is trivia. This capstone is the direct test: real numbers that didn't exist when this course was written, a real RBI statement, and a blank page. If you can fill that page with a coherent, mechanism-based thesis, you have what the course promised on Day 1 — you will read macro news and understand *why* markets move, instead of being surprised by them.

This is not a quiz with an answer key. It's the actual job. Do it for real.

## Part 2 — Write the thesis (25 min)

Produce **one page (~400–500 words)**, structured exactly as below. This structure *is* the course. Every section must state a **mechanism**, not just a direction.

```markdown
## India Macro Thesis — [date]

### 1. The setup (what's the data saying?)
- Growth: [rising/falling? cite the pulse — PMI, IIP, GST, GDP mix]
- Inflation: [headline vs. core; demand-pull or cost-push? cite Day 9]
- Policy: [repo, stance, latest MPC signal; what did the RBI just change? Day 23]
- Markets: [10y yield, curve shape, USD/INR, spreads, VIX]

### 2. The surprise (Day 3, 22)
What most recently came in vs. expectations, and how was the market positioned?
The mover is the surprise, not the level.

### 3. The regime (Day 21)
Which of the four boxes are we in — and, more importantly, which are we
transitioning *toward*? Cross-check curve (Day 11) and spreads (Day 20).

### 4. The mechanism → asset read (Days 13–20)
For EACH — with a one-line mechanism, not just a direction:
- Equities (Nifty + which sectors): [why]
- Bonds (10y G-sec / duration): [why]
- Rupee (USD/INR): [why — include the Fed/oil overlay, Day 19]
- Credit: [why]

### 5. The global overlay (Days 19, 23)
What the Fed, US yields, oil, and flows add to or subtract from the domestic story.

### 6. The risks (Days 18, 24, 25)
- The narrative I might be falling for: [name it]
- The positioning that could unwind: [name it]
- The reflexive break that would invalidate this: [name it]

### 7. The falsifier
The single data point or event that would prove this thesis wrong.
```

## Part 3 — Stress-test it (10 min)

Before you call it done, attack your own thesis with the course's sharpest tools:

- **Mechanism check (Day 24):** For each asset call, did you delete a step? Is any call really a lazy narrative ("cuts are bullish," "strong GDP good," "RBI hiked so rupee up")? Put the deleted step back.
- **Sign-flip check (Days 15, 19):** For the rupee and for equities-on-rate-moves, did you check *why* rates moved and *whose* rates moved?
- **Regime-sensitivity check (Days 21, 22):** Is the market currently more afraid of weak growth or high inflation? Does your "good news / bad news" sign match the regime?
- **Positioning check (Days 18, 22):** Is your call already the consensus (and thus priced)? Where's the *variant* view?
- **Fragility check (Day 25):** Are you long risk into complacency (tight spreads, low VIX, one-way narrative)? Is your position size survivable if the regime snaps?
- **Feynman check:** Could a smart friend from another field follow your thesis over coffee? If any sentence needs jargon you can't unpack, rewrite it.

## What "done" looks like

A single page that:
1. Identifies the **surprise**, not just the level.
2. Names the **regime** and the transition risk.
3. Gives a **mechanism** for every asset call — a colleague could disagree with your *judgment* but not find a *logic gap*.
4. Includes the **global overlay** (this is India — the Fed and oil are never absent).
5. States **where you're wrong** and the **falsifier**.

If a colleague could read it and place a trade — or argue with your judgment on its merits rather than its logic — you're done. That is the investor this course set out to make.

## Where to go from here

- **Repeat it monthly.** Do this exact exercise every CPI print and every MPC meeting. The dashboard is built; the muscle grows with reps. Compare each month's thesis to what actually happened — that feedback loop is how judgment compounds.
- **Keep the callbacks alive.** Spaced retrieval didn't end on Day 26. Every real data release is a live callback to Days 3, 13, 17, 21. Use them or lose them.
- **Deepen selectively.** When a specific area bites — the rupee, credit, the RBI's reasoning — go to the [bibliography](../../../bibliography.md) and read the one source mapped to it. You now have the scaffolding to read those works *actively*, with questions, not passively.
- **Watch yourself (Day 18).** The hardest bias to catch is your own. Keep a note of theses that were wrong *and why* — narrative capture, positioning misread, regime error. That log is worth more than any book.

## The promise, delivered

On Day 1, a macro headline was weather — something that happened *to* your portfolio. Today you can take that same headline, isolate the surprise, trace it through the mechanism, locate the regime, read it across four assets, overlay the global tide, and name where you might be wrong. You understand *why* markets move on macro news. You will still be wrong often — everyone is — but you will rarely be *surprised* in the way you were on Day 1. That was the whole point.

> **Transfer — the rest of your investing life:** The next time the RBI meets or CPI prints, you won't reach for a pundit's take. You'll open your dashboard, find the surprise, and write the chain yourself. Input: the news. Today's idea: the entire machine. Output: your own defensible view. Keep doing it.
