<details>
<summary>Compare to these</summary>

1. **Discount-rate lens (17):** Every asset = future cash flows discounted at `r = r_f + risk premium`; `Price = C/(r−g)`, so rates, growth, and fear price everything.
2. **Surprise (3):** Prices embed expectations, so they move on actual − consensus, not the level.
3. **Transmission (14):** A repo change reaches the economy via interest-rate, credit, asset-price, exchange-rate, and expectations channels — slowly and leakily in India.
4. **Four regimes (21):** Growth × inflation → Goldilocks / overheating / stagflation / disinflation-bust, each favouring different assets.
5. **CPI vs WPI (9):** CPI (retail, ~46% food) is the RBI's 4%±2% legal target; core strips food/fuel; WPI reads margins.
6. **Model breaks (25):** Reflexivity, regime snaps, and contagion make the mechanism amplify/jam/reverse at extremes — hold the model *and* its limits.
7. **Transactions (1):** Spending ≡ income ≡ output; the economy is one closed loop.
8. **The rupee (19):** USD/INR moves on the India–US rate/growth *differential*, oil, and herding FII flows — the global valve.
9. **Repo & G-secs (10):** The repo is the anchor price of money; bond prices move inversely to yields; the G-sec yield underpins all valuation.
10. **Reading the RBI (23):** The rate is usually priced; the market trades the change in stance, projections, vote, and language — the path, not the point.
11. **Trend vs cycle (2):** Long-run growth (supply) ≠ short-run cycle (demand); investors trade the cycle.
12. **Credit spreads (20):** Corporate yield minus G-sec = the price of default risk; widening spreads lead equities into downturns.
13. **Reaction function (13):** The MPC moves the repo in response to the inflation gap and output gap — so data predicts the RBI.
14. **Behavioral engine (18):** Anchoring, extrapolation, herding, and loss aversion make price over- and undershoot fair value.
</details>

## Step 2 — Re-derive the full macro-to-asset chain (12 min)

Fill every blank from memory. This is the entire course as one sentence-chain:

```
Someone changes their spending → total spending = total [__A] (Day 1),
measured as GDP = C + [__B] + G + NX, whose swing engine is component [__C] (Day 7).
If spending outruns goods → [__D], measured in India as [__E] (~46% food), targeted at [__F]%.
The RBI reads inflation & growth via its [__G] function (Day 13) and moves the [__H] rate,
which transmits — slowly in India — through channels to the economy (Day 14).
That rate anchors the risk-free [__I] yield, which (with growth and a risk premium)
sets the [__J] rate that prices every asset via Price = C / (r − [__K]) (Day 17).
But markets only move on the [__L] versus expectations (Day 3),
and the human crowd makes price [__M]-shoot and [__N]-shoot around fair value (Day 18).
The prevailing growth × inflation [__O] (Day 21) decides which assets win,
the [__P] connects India to the world via flows and oil (Day 19),
and at extremes the whole model [__Q] through reflexive loops (Day 25).
```

<details>
<summary>Filled-in chain</summary>

A = income · B = I (investment) · C = investment (I) · D = inflation · E = CPI · F = 4 · G = reaction · H = repo · I = G-sec · J = discount · K = g (growth) · L = surprise · M = over · N = under · O = regime · P = rupee · Q = breaks
</details>

**Mechanism questions (answer without looking — these probe *why*):**
1. Why does an unexpected RBI hike hurt a high-growth stock *more* than a dividend-paying value stock?
2. Why can the rupee *fall* even when the RBI *raises* rates?
3. Why do widening credit spreads often appear *before* the equity market falls?
4. Why does the RBI sometimes *look through* a 6% CPI print instead of hiking?
5. Why can an *emergency* rate cut make the market *fall*?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The growth stock is long-duration — its value is distant future cash flows, which a higher discount rate shrinks most (Day 17). 
2. What matters is the India–US rate *differential*; if the Fed hiked more, the gap narrowed and money flowed out (Days 19, 24). 
3. Credit investors have asymmetric payoffs (capped upside, total downside), so they react to deterioration first — an early-warning lead (Day 20). 
4. If the inflation is supply-driven (food/fuel), with core contained, hiking won't fix supply and would crush growth — so it looks through it (Days 9, 13). 
5. The lower `r_f` is swamped by a spiking risk premium and collapsing growth expectations — the cut signals danger (Days 17, 18, 24, 25).
</details>

## Step 4 — Quiz (6 min)

> **Why scrambled and cross-cutting:** these deliberately span modules. Answer each cold.

1. Diversification (bonds hedging stocks) fails in certain downturns. Name the regime where it fails and explain the mechanism. *(Days 21, 25)*
2. A company beats earnings and the stock falls. One-word cause, one-sentence why. *(Day 3)*
3. Tech stocks with unchanged businesses halved when rates rose. Explain via one equation. *(Day 17)*
4. The RBI cuts rates (dovish) but the rupee is collapsing because the Fed turned hawkish. What is the RBI likely *forced* to do, and why does its dovish domestic view get overridden? *(Days 19 + 13)*
5. **Cross-concept:** India's CPI is ~46% food, the RBI legally targets *headline* CPI, and the market is currently inflation-obsessed. A bad monsoon spikes CPI to 6.5%. Trace the full chain to the Nifty, and explain how three different concepts (inflation type, reaction function, regime) *interact* to determine whether stocks fall. *(Days 9 + 13 + 21)*

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. **Stagflation** (growth↓, inflation↑) — and inflation scares generally. High inflation keeps `r_f` up (bonds fall) while weak growth hurts earnings (equities fall), so both drop *together*; the "bonds hedge stocks" negative correlation only holds in *disinflationary* downturns where the RBI can cut (Days 21, 25).
2. **Surprise** — the market expected *more* than the beat, so the surprise was negative despite good news (Day 3).
3. `Price = C/(r−g)`: rising `r` (discount rate) shrinks the present value of distant future cash flows most, and tech is long-duration — so unchanged businesses lost value purely from the higher `r` (Day 17).
4. The RBI is likely **forced to pause or defend the rupee** (hold/hike or spend reserves) despite wanting to cut, because a widening US–India rate gap is driving outflows and rupee weakness (Day 19); currency defence and the rate differential override the domestic dovish view — global constrains domestic (Days 19, 13).
5. **The chain:** bad monsoon → food prices spike → headline CPI to 6.5% (above the band). **Interaction of three concepts:** (i) *inflation type* (Day 9) — it's supply-driven/cost-push, so mechanically the RBI *could* look through it; **but** (ii) the *reaction function* (Day 13) targets *headline*, and if the spike threatens to unanchor expectations or leak into core, the RBI must lean hawkish; and (iii) the *regime* (Day 21) — because the market is *currently inflation-obsessed*, its sensitivity to this print is high, so it prices a hawkish RBI response fast. Net: even though the inflation is "just food," the headline-targeting mandate + the inflation-focused regime mean the market expects higher rates → higher discount rate → **Nifty falls** (rate-sensitives worst). Had the regime been growth-focused and core clearly contained, the same print might have been shrugged off. The *interaction* — not any single concept — determines the outcome.
</details>
